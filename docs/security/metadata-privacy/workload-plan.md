# Workload qualification plan — 2026-11-02

This revises the test model, not the historical outcomes or application security gates. The original master specification §13.3 calls for 20 delivered messages/s **burst**, 100 online users, 500-browser/WebSocket connections, five concurrent uploads and 30% intermittent users. It does not require five independent online cryptographic devices per account or sustained all-nine-recipient completion before each next send. The individual matrix cases isolate costs; passing them separately does not establish the combined specification workload. A combined run and reference-host qualification remain distinct requirements.

## Fixed definitions

- A trusted device is an approved cryptographic identity; it may be offline. A connection is a live authenticated socket; multiple tabs may share one device identity. Neither is a person or a new human message.
- The normal population is 100 synthetic accounts, two pre-enrolled conversation-member devices per account. One primary is online for every user and 20 secondary devices are also online: 200 trusted identities, 120 connected identities initially. These are initial product assumptions, not measured user statistics or a new application device limit.
- Mixed conversations partition users into 40 direct conversations (80 users) and four five-person groups (20 users). Dedicated fan-out runs use groups of 5, 10 and 25 people. All devices join before measurements; offline devices retain their eligibility and pending ciphertext.
- A returning or newly opened device first retrieves and durably saves the history at its connection snapshot before becoming eligible to author. Other ready users maintain the offered service-wide rate. Planned return restores the same devices that were active before disconnection; it does not silently switch a laptop user back to a cold phone. Readiness latency and interrupted delivery remain visible.
- Traffic is open-loop, service-wide new application messages. Every eligible online person receives one author turn per fixed interleaved cycle; that person’s ready devices rotate within their turn. A second connected device does not double a person’s outgoing message rate. Authors respect existing per-account limits. Sender ordering and each receiving device's MLS processing remain sequential. Sending does not wait for every recipient's ACK.
- Receivers discover changes through real authenticated WebSocket hints and bounded periodic HTTP sync. Catch-up fetches pages of up to 100 actual envelopes, verifies signatures/hash chain/MLS sender/roster/epoch/plaintext, fsyncs encrypted native state, then ACKs only the highest contiguous saved sequence. Idle sync sends no unchanged ACK.
- Server acceptance, distinct messages completed by every continuously connected recipient, continuously online per-device durable delivery, interrupted/offline catch-up, control events and recipient copies are separate measurements. An offline device is never reported delivered until it actually saves and acknowledges its messages.

## Gates and evidence

The mandatory acceptance budget remains p95 POST-to-durable-response <750 ms on a qualified reference host. Initial local engineering budgets additionally use p95 continuously-online verified/saved delivery ≤3 seconds, sender/continuous-online unfinished work ≤10 seconds, and complete final deferred catch-up within a bounded drain. The 3-second online budget is a proposed engineering budget, not a pre-existing specification guarantee. Planned offline intervals are recorded separately and never removed from recovery results.

Every offered message must either have its exact durable acceptance and complete expected recipient ledger, or the run fails. No accepted-message loss, unauthorized delivery, chain/MLS/plaintext mismatch, cursor regression, ACK-before-checkpoint, unexpected socket error, hidden 429 or DB deadlock is a pass. The final encrypted native checkpoint of every device is restored and inspected. Repeated reconnects and control epochs must not corrupt state. Security, synchronous commit, fsync, storage quotas, source throttles and PostgreSQL's existing connection limit remain effective.

Native clients are not physical browsers. Earlier Chromium/Firefox suites cover real WASM/browser flows separately; physical phones, domestic networks and reference-production hardware remain unqualified. The shared workstation co-locates API/database, crypto clients and checkpoint I/O; report that scope and never infer a universal server ceiling. Do not run validation/build/other load jobs during measured capacity windows. The old and new API comparisons use the same new driver, population, rates, native binary and worker count; revised workloads are not compared directly with the previous 14/s drained overload as a claimed speedup.

## Primary research behind the model

[Signal linked-device mailboxes](https://signal.org/blog/a-synchronized-start-for-linked-devices/) separate online receipt from later device retrieval and upload encrypted attachments once. [SimpleX SMP](https://github.com/simplex-chat/simplexmq/blob/stable/protocol/simplex-messaging.md#acknowledge-message-delivery) specifies asynchronous queues and acknowledgment after local storage. [SimpleX channel delivery](https://simplex.chat/docs/protocol/channels-protocol.html#delivery-pipeline) uses persistent tasks/jobs, batching and paginated fan-out; it is a different protocol and not a benchmark for HAVEN. [SimpleX device-linking FAQ](https://simplex.chat/faq/#can-i-use-the-same-profile-on-desktop-do-messages-sync-cross-platform) describes a different multi-device model. Adopt useful scheduling/measurement principles without replacing HAVEN's MLS, trust model or domestic-only runtime.

## Notification implementation and test revisions

The outbox worker now owns one asyncio loop and Redis backend for its lifetime. It coalesces the account-scoped `sync.hint` notifications of up to 100 claimed outbox rows, then marks the whole batch sent in one update only after every target send succeeds. A partial notification failure rolls back every claimed row; duplicate retry hints are allowed, and HTTP synchronization remains authoritative. Nonempty batches drain immediately; only idle/failure cycles pause 0.5 seconds. This does not batch or skip durable application envelopes, MLS processing, checkpoints or ACK authority.

Initial v2 matrix results are retained at the evidence root, including narrow recovery-latency failure and the 82-minute interrupted endurance target. The v2.1 revisions add catch-up-before-author readiness, preservation of the actual active device on reconnect, distinct complete-message accounting and structured backlog stages. v2.2 adds the combined profile and diagnostics for the actual oldest pending recipients, plus explicit rekey-stage attribution; it does not alter existing workload rates or thresholds. Performance thresholds are unchanged. New labelled output directories refuse to overwrite existing case results. `matrix-summary.json` status COMPLETED means the selected cases ran; each case's integrity and performance fields determine success.

Run `apps/api/.venv/bin/python scripts/qualify-metadata-workloads.py --label <new-label>` for the complete serial matrix. Use `--cases` to select cases. Never run two invocations concurrently; each requires exclusive ownership of the benchmark's loopback ports. A full two-hour result must complete all 14,400 planned application messages at 2/s; a shorter stopped run is not a soak pass. Independent deferred devices are measured at final actual reconnection, including their entire offline wait. Do not apply the online delivery budget to intentional offline time.

## Author scheduling and storage scope

The v2 device cycle used account order. Because conversation users were adjacent, it concentrated each group's authors into a wave once per cycle, rather than interleaving independently active people. v3 uses one fixed seed (20261102, not searched or tuned) to interleave the same device slots. Every eligible device retains one opportunity per cycle, group weights and total rates remain unchanged, and no offered intent is dropped. The legacy-stress driver is unchanged. Versioned outcomes and exact fingerprints preserve the old traffic pattern and its failures. Structured per-send stage durations distinguish queueing, synchronization, native encryption, checkpoints, POST and ACK; they are diagnostics rather than exemptions from the same ten-second gate.

Read-only host inspection shows both client checkpoint files and Docker database storage on the root ext4 partition of a USB-attached TwinMOS SSD using `usb-storage`. The separate NVMe Windows partitions are not mounted or modified. This co-locates many simulated devices' durable writes with the server's WAL flushes; actual PostgreSQL WAL/lock samples and checkpoint timings record those costs. The interface name or advertised USB speed does not prove a device throughput ceiling or prove that disk is the only remaining cause. Keep fsync and synchronous commit enabled. Do not move state to tmpfs, mount another partition or change kernel/storage settings to manufacture a pass. Reference-host and distributed-client qualification remain separate from this rig.

## Final v5 recovery coverage

The final author policy is person-based (introduced in v4), superseding the historical v2/v3 device-based cycles. The fixed account permutation still uses seed 20261102; this choice was not searched against outcomes. v5 chooses 30% intermittent accounts using a separate fixed stream while retaining at least one online person in every affected conversation. This corrects a coverage gap in earlier runs, which disconnected whole direct-chat pairs and therefore did not require new messages to queue for those participants.

The driver snapshots the devices actually online before the planned outage. It records new application messages/copies accepted for those devices while disconnected and counts their actual verified, saved and ACKed receipt later. Full intermittent, combined and endurance cases require **every affected conversation** to have an actual new offline backlog, completed offline/return actions, and equality between queued and durably received copies. A short smoke is explicitly exempt from full-window coverage, not described as recovery qualification. All existing online/POST/backlog thresholds remain in effect. Earlier connectivity-only passes retain their measured status but do not meet this stronger coverage criterion.

The final serial matrix is in the `final-v5` evidence directory. Intermediate `v21` and `final-v4` runs were intentionally interrupted before their long soak while correcting author/recovery definitions. Their completed case JSON remains; their summaries do not say COMPLETED. No interrupted baseline or endurance is treated as a pass. Only individually versioned final-source case outcomes support the final assessment.

## Independent author ACK pipeline (v6)

Structured traces exposed another generator dependency: the conversation lane included the previous author's final ACK roundtrip. That ACK is required for the author's own durable cursor, but is not a prerequisite for another device to publish the next valid envelope. v6 bounds each conversation to two author workers. It holds the conversation lane through actual verified acceptance and encrypted sender checkpoint, then releases it while retaining the individual author's lock through its real ACK. The next author's native catch-up/encryption/POST can overlap that ACK. Control transitions retain their ordered owner handling.

The main future still covers the entire intent through ACK, and the unchanged ten-second oldest-intent gate still includes queueing, checkpoint, POST and ACK. Final checks additionally require every sender checkpoint and an empty pending-author-ACK ledger. No message is considered complete merely because the publication lane was released. `--sender-workers 1` retains the serialization control.

Two real-native diagnostic cases use `--hold-first-sender-ack`: the first application ACK waits for a second publication or a five-second timeout. With one worker the second author cannot release that hold; with two workers it does, while all signatures, MLS state, durable cursors and restored checkpoints remain correct. Both probes pass and are explicitly marked diagnostic, with actual timings rather than capacity evidence. Capacity profiles never enable the hold. See `v6-probes` and the final `final-v6` matrix. Earlier `final-v5` cases remain valid for their source/definition but its planned long soak was interrupted to correct this proven test dependency.

Lifecycle changes drain already-scheduled outbound intents and finish device critical sections before controlled disconnection. They test queued new messages and real reconnect/catch-up; they do not claim hard-power-loss or packet-loss-during-checkpoint qualification. Browser failover, transport retry and physical-device gates retain their separate scopes.

## Natural tabs versus constant occupancy (v6.2)

`combined-natural` retains each device’s initial share of 500 tabs. Planned disconnection removes those connections; the other devices keep their original tabs. Returning devices restore their previous share. The measured natural case therefore has 500 → 356 → 500 connections during its outage and passes all local gates, with 1,300 applications and 5,352 recipient copies verified. The existing `combined` case preserves its deliberate constant-500 redistribution, which creates additional tab churn on the remaining online identities. Its failures remain stress evidence; it is not the ordinary outage model. All other profile policies and thresholds are unchanged. Thirteen unit checks verify the final definitions, including fixed allocation and redacted failure provenance.

The [results report](workload-results.md) binds the current passing local case, all retained failures and the actual endurance outcome. Case execution spans independently isolated labelled runs; per-case versions and component hashes, rather than one overall all-pass claim, define what was measured.
